WATER SYSTEMS

What makes a reliable home water system?

If you want dependable water at home, every part of the system needs to work together.

1. Start with the water source

The first question is where the water is coming from. Depending on the property, this may be a mains supply, borehole, well, or another source.

The source affects the rest of the system. You need to understand the available supply before deciding on storage or pumping.

2. Think about storage

Many properties use a water storage tank so that water is available when the supply is interrupted or demand temporarily increases.

The appropriate tank capacity depends on factors such as the number of people using the property, the type of property, and how regularly water is available.

3. Consider pressure

Having water in a tank does not necessarily mean you will have strong pressure at every tap.

Tank position, pipe sizes, distance, elevation, and the use of a pump can all affect the final pressure.

4. Plan the complete system

A good installation considers the whole system instead of treating each component separately.

If you are unsure what your property needs, it is better to assess the situation before buying a pump, tank, or other equipment.

WATER PUMPS

Why is your water pressure so low?

Before replacing your pump, there are several things worth checking.

1. The pump may not be the problem

Low pressure can come from restrictions elsewhere in the system. Blocked pipes, unsuitable pipe sizes, valves, filters, and leaks can all affect water flow.

2. Check the water source

A pump cannot supply water that is not reaching it properly. If the tank is empty, the source is unreliable, or the inlet has a problem, changing the pump may not solve the issue.

3. Pump size matters

Bigger does not automatically mean better. A pump should be selected according to the requirements of the property and the system it is expected to serve.

4. Pipework affects pressure

Water has to travel through the plumbing before it reaches your shower, tap, or other outlet. Long pipe runs, bends, elevation changes, and unsuitable pipework can affect performance.

5. When should you get professional help?

If pressure suddenly drops, the pump becomes unusually noisy, frequently switches on and off, or stops supplying water properly, professional inspection can help identify the actual cause before equipment is replaced.

Don't replace a pump blindly.

Find out what is causing the pressure problem first. The right solution depends on the complete system.

BOREHOLES

Thinking about drilling a borehole? Start here.

A borehole can be a valuable water source, but good planning matters before drilling.

What is a borehole?

A borehole is a drilled opening into the ground designed to access underground water. Once developed and equipped appropriately, it can become part of a property's water supply system.

Is a borehole suitable for every property?

Not necessarily. Underground conditions vary from one location to another, so a borehole project should begin with proper assessment and planning.

What should you consider before drilling?

Think about the intended use of the water, expected demand, available space, access for equipment, and the complete system that will be needed after drilling.

Drilling is only the beginning

A completed borehole still needs the right pumping and storage arrangement. Depending on the property, this may include a submersible pump, storage tank, control equipment, and suitable pipework.

What about water quality?

Underground water should not be assumed to have the same quality everywhere. If the water will be used for drinking or other sensitive purposes, appropriate testing and treatment decisions are important.

Plan the complete system.

Don't think only about getting water underground. Think about how that water will reach the people and outlets that need it.

WATER QUALITY

Clear water does not tell the whole story.

Water appearance is only one part of understanding water quality.

Why can water look clean but still need treatment?

Some water-quality problems cannot be identified simply by looking at the water. Different sources can contain different substances or characteristics that require appropriate testing and treatment.

Common signs worth paying attention to

Unusual colour, smell, taste, staining, sediment, or recurring problems with water can be reasons to investigate further. These signs do not by themselves identify the exact cause.

Treatment should match the problem

There is no single treatment system that is automatically right for every property. The source and the actual water-quality issue should guide the choice.

Test before choosing equipment

If water quality is a concern, testing can provide useful information before investing in treatment equipment.

Don't guess your treatment system.

Identify the water-quality problem first, then choose an appropriate solution.

WATER STORAGE

How much water storage does your property need?

The right storage setup depends on how your property uses water and how reliable the incoming supply is.

Why water storage matters

A storage tank can provide a reserve when the incoming supply is unavailable or insufficient. It can also form an important part of a pumped water system.

Don't choose a tank by size alone

A larger tank is not automatically the best choice. The number of people using the property, the type of property, available space, water availability, and expected demand should all be considered.

Where should the tank be installed?

Tank position can affect both the practical operation of the system and the pressure available to outlets. The supporting structure also needs to be suitable for the weight of a full tank.

Storage and pressure are different things

Having plenty of water in a tank does not automatically mean that showers and taps will have strong pressure.

The height of the tank, pipework, elevation, and whether a pump is used can all affect the pressure experienced at the outlets.

Keep stored water protected

Tanks should be properly covered and maintained so that the stored water is protected from unnecessary contamination and other problems.

Plan storage around actual demand.

The best tank setup is one that fits the property and the way the water system will actually be used.

MAINTENANCE

Simple maintenance can prevent bigger water problems.

You do not have to wait until the system completely fails before paying attention to it.

Pay attention to changes

If water pressure changes suddenly, a pump starts behaving differently, a tank begins leaking, or you notice unusual sounds, don't simply ignore it.

Check visible pipework

Where pipework is accessible, look for obvious leaks, damaged connections, unusual moisture, or signs that water is escaping.

Keep tanks and surrounding areas clean

The area around water storage equipment should be kept reasonably clean and the tank itself should be appropriately maintained.

Don't ignore your pump

Unusual noise, repeated cycling, reduced performance, or failure to deliver water can indicate that a pump or another part of the system needs attention.

Know when to call a professional

Basic observation can help you notice a problem, but repairs and electrical or mechanical work should be handled appropriately rather than guessed at.

Small warning signs matter.

Identifying a problem early can help you understand what needs attention before the situation becomes more disruptive.

QUICK ANSWERS

Questions property owners often ask.

How do I know what size water tank I need?

It depends on factors such as the number of people using the property, daily water demand, the reliability of the incoming supply, and the space available for the tank. A proper assessment is better than choosing a size based only on what another property uses.

Why is my tank full but my shower pressure is weak?

A full tank does not guarantee strong pressure. Tank height, pipe size, pipe length, elevation, valves, restrictions, and pumping arrangements can all affect the pressure at the shower.

Should I buy a bigger water pump?

Not necessarily. A bigger pump may not solve the actual problem and may be unsuitable for the system. The cause of the low flow or pressure should be identified first.

Is borehole water automatically safe to drink?

You should not assume that water is safe simply because it comes from a borehole. Water quality can vary, so appropriate testing and treatment decisions are important when drinking water is involved.

How often should a water system be checked?

The appropriate maintenance schedule depends on the equipment and how the system is used. More importantly, unusual changes in pressure, sound, leaks, water quality, or pump behaviour should not be ignored.
